Day 2 — Ball in Peril

Key rule: every player records normal individual Stableford points on every hole. The selected pink-ball player’s points also count towards the team’s live Day 2 total.

How the pink ball works

THREE PINK BALLSONE IN PLAYLIVE SCORING
Each team starts with three.The scorekeeper selects which player uses it on each hole.That player’s Stableford points are added automatically to the team total.

Balls lost and penalties

LOSTWHAT HAPPENSCUMULATIVE PENALTY
1First reserve becomes the pink ball.−5 team points
2Final reserve becomes the pink ball.−10 team points
3No pink ball remains; no further pink-ball points.−15 team points

Every loss deducts 5 points from the team’s Day 2 score automatically. It never reduces the player’s individual Stableford score.

For example, a team starting Day 2 on 3 points and scoring 25 pink-ball Stableford points with one loss will have an overall total of 23 points: 3 + 25 − 5.

Day 3 — Nick Faldo Singles

All eight players play individual Stableford off their playing handicaps on the Nick Faldo Course at Amendoeira.

The live Day 3 ranking awards 7 points for first place, then 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 and 0. Each player’s position points are added to their team’s overall score. Tied players share the average of the points for the positions they occupy. Because the ranking updates hole by hole, the team lead can change throughout the round.

The Rusty Wedge

The Rusty Wedge is the individual competition. A player’s Day 2 Palmares Stableford score is added to their Day 3 Faldo Stableford score. The highest combined total wins the Rusty Wedge.

Ball in Peril team scoring is separate and never replaces or reduces anyone’s normal individual Day 2 Stableford score.
